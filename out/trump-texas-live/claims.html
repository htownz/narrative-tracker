factcheckablemisleading
why
When the claim was first posted on Oct 8, the Pentagon had announced the plan. The Defense Department said in a statement that the execution of Nidal Malik Hasan would be live-streamed. It later confirmed to NPR that the execution "will be livestreamed," with details to follow. The claim states this as settled, though, and the plan is now in doubt. The Hill reports that the White House has signalled a potential reversal after bipartisan outcry. Trump said he "wasn't involved in that decision" to livestream, and that he will "make a decision ultimately." Axios reported that he privately told a confidant it "might not happen." Vice President Vance said he doesn't know that it will happen. Separately, Snopes notes the Army has no regulations explicitly allowing livestreamed executions, though experts say it could change or waive them. Hasan could also delay the execution for years with a federal habeas appeal. The announced plan is accurate, but "will be streamed live" overstates how firm it is. That makes the claim misleading.
