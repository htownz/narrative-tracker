I don’t like to admit this, but I wasn’t born in Austin. We moved to Austin in 1984 when I was four years old, after my father got a call from a headhunter and agreed to leave the largest labor law firm in the Midwest and our nice life in Cincinnati to join to the Law Department at Motorola, which was a truly great American company at the time. My father bought a house my mother had never seen, in a state they’d never visited or knew anyone (other than a distant cousin), and even then the infinite sacrifice of my parents was evident for my sister Claire (18 months younger than me) and I. Even though my Dad initially worked pretty far away at the Ed Bluestein location (on South 183 not far from where the airport is now), he decided to buy a home in the Round Rock ISD, with the belief that the schools would be better than AISD by the time Claire and I needed them. It’s amazing how prescient that decision was, in retrospect. Claire and I both attended National Blue Ribbon elementary (Laurel Mountain), middle (Canyon Vista) and high (Westwood) schools. You can read about how unlikely that is here (https://t.co/NdD2iWh1Gz) (TL:DR: It’s basically a 0.0034% chance in the U.S. to attend all three). My childhood was idyllic. We were not wealthy, but we were comfortable. My father is the first person to attend and graduate from college in his family. His father worked three jobs to put him through Lehigh University (a baby Ivy known for their engineering school). My mother’s parents both attended and graduated from the University of Pittsburgh (#HailToPitt) and my mother graduated from Mount Union College in Alliance, OH. They both grew up in Pittsburgh (the cause of my intense #HereWeGo Steelers devotion), and went to rival high schools, meeting on a blind date and dating long distance throughout college in a very sweet love story. After college, Dad immediately became an officer in the Coast Guard, so they got married on 12/26/70 as he had one day off from Coast Guard duties, and this year will celebrate their 56th (!!!) wedding anniversary. My parents ran a tight ship. How cool was I in high school? My mother ran the sober prom committee. Need I say more? My basketball season my senior year was akin to the film Hoosiers. Westwood had never won more than one playoff game in school history. That season, we improbably won four playoff games, decisively defeating two-time State Champion and undefeated #1 ranked Dallas Carter at the Ferrell Center in Waco. We ended up losing in the state semifinal to Midland Lee and my athletic career came to an end. I did have a consequential performance against Tomball in our first round playoff game, scoring 7 points in four minutes and erasing a 16 point deficit in the 3rd quarter. We won in overtime on a buzzer beater and our playoff run continued. I was carried off the court with people chanting my name, a truly bizarre, yet memorable experience. I spent part of my spring visiting colleges with my father. My top list of schools was: Notre Dame, Northwestern, Washington & Lee, and Rhodes College. I was rejected by all. My fallbacks (arrogant term, I know) were: Lehigh University (my Dad’s alma mater), UT and Texas A&M. I got into all three. I might have been able to walk on to the Lehigh basketball team, but was afraid of the brutal winters, isolated campus, and uphill walk everyday (literally). Texas A&M didn’t seem a fit for me. So I reluctantly accepted UT, mildly depressed that I was not ‘going away for school’. Almost overnight, I began having what would become an extraordinary experience in my five (victory lap!) years at UT. I joined a mid-size blue collar fraternity (Delta Chi), served as pledge class president, rush chair, social chair, and President for two years. I was Interfraternity Council (IFC) President. I was accepted into UT’s most elite men’s service and leadership organization, Texas Cowboys. I ran two successful Student Body President campaigns. And I was appointed to the President’s Student Advisory Committee (PSAC), and got to know and remain friends with the legendary former UT President Dr. Larry Faulker (we both attend Covenant Presbyterian with our brides).
