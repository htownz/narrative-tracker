Nearly half of the 80 school districts in the Houston-Galveston region may lose a combined $68 million in funding.
factcheckableagainstsupported
why
The claim repeats the opening sentence of a Houston Chronicle exclusive. That sentence says nearly half of the 80 school districts in the Houston-Galveston region may lose a combined $68 million in funding after some 8,000 students left for Texas vouchers. The figures come from an estimate by anti-voucher groups. The report was produced by Every Texan and the Texas Center for School Voucher Transparency. The groups based it on average state funding of about $8,200 per student. The report counts about 8,100 students and calls $68M the maximum, so the "may lose" wording is accurate. The claim leaves out that the loss is attributed to vouchers (Texas Education Freedom Accounts). Read in the context of HISD, TEA takeovers and A-F ratings, it could suggest a different cause. About 40% of Texas high school graduates are not college ready.
factcheckableagainstmisleading
why
The "about 40%" figure depends on which measure and which year are used, and the claim names neither. A TEA-commissioned CCMR indicator study found that almost 40% of 2022 graduates did not demonstrate college, career, or military readiness . That is a combined measure, not college readiness alone. In the same study, 56.8% of 2022 graduates met the college-ready standard , so about 43% did not. The newer TEA-based figure is lower. Understanding Houston reports that the statewide college-ready average rose from 50% in 2018 to 70% in 2024 , which implies about 30% not college ready. Kinder Institute reporting says the rise came largely from college prep courses, which went from 3% of graduates in 2019 to 20% in 2024 , while other measures barely moved. Stricter measures give much higher "not ready" shares. Texas 2036 reported that SAT/ACT college readiness fell from 28% in 2018 to 23% in 2022 , which means roughly three-quarters were not ready by that test. Fort Worth Report (Oct. 13, 2026) found that in Tarrant County 82% were considered college-ready, dropping to 51% without college-preparatory courses . The roughly 40% figure fits only the 2022 TEA data. It is not a settled [1] [2] [3] [4]
