SpaceX has approval to launch and operate 15,000 next-generation satellites for Starlink Mobile.
factcheckablesupported
why
The FCC's Space Bureau issued order DA 26-1078 on October 6, 2026. It granted SpaceX's September 2025 application in part. Trade outlets report that the FCC approved SpaceX's application to launch and operate 15,000 next-generation satellites for Starlink Mobile and granted a waiver letting it sell satellite wireless service without leasing spectrum from a terrestrial carrier . The claim's wording matches this reporting. Two caveats: the approval is conditional, with decisions on parts of the spectrum request deferred , and deployment is expected to begin in 2027 and depends on Starship . The 15,000 figure is easy to confuse with the separate January 2026 grant. In that grant, the FCC authorized an additional 7,500 Gen2 Starlink satellites, bringing that system's total to 15,000 . The October approval is a different constellation. I could not retrieve the order text itself, and one secondary source says the same. The finding rests on consistent trade reporting that cites the order number, plus the FCC's January release for the distinction. The claim has no direct Texas link beyond the broader satellite-versus-fiber context. [1] [2] [3] [4]

The FCC issued SpaceX a waiver to offer satellite wireless service without a leasing agreement with a ground-based mobile operator.
factcheckablesupported
why
The claim matches the reporting. TechSpot says the FCC cleared the mobile application on October 6 and also granted a waiver letting SpaceX offer satellite wireless services without a spectrum-leasing agreement with a terrestrial mobile operator . Teslanorth, citing the order, says the FCC granted SpaceX's application in part in order DA 26-1078, adopted and released October 6, 2026 . It also notes that the FCC waived the rule requiring a spectrum leasing deal with a ground-based mobile operator . 24/7 Wall St. gives the FCC's reasoning, which is that requiring SpaceX to lease spectrum to itself would be an unnecessary regulatory burden . This follows SpaceX's purchase of EchoStar spectrum. Yahoo Finance adds that the order came from an FCC bureau rather than the full commission, so it can still be reviewed . That caveat doesn't make the claim inaccurate. I did not open the FCC order itself, so this rests on secondary reporting that cites it. The claim also has little direct connection to Texas, but it is accurate as worded. [1] [2] [3] [4]
