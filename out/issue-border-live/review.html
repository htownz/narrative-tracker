ALERT: 🚨 Federal court blocks Trump administration border wall in Texas’ Big Bend region. On Friday, October 2, 2026, U.S. District Judge Kathleen Cardone of the Western District of Texas in El Paso granted a preliminary injunction in a case filed by the Center for Biological Diversity, Friends of the Ruidosa Church, and a local river guide and landowner. The order says the Department of Homeland Security, Secretary Markwayne Mullin, and Customs and Border Protection may not build border barriers or related infrastructure, or do construction or ground-disturbing work, in the Big Bend sector unless they comply with all applicable laws. Cardone found the plaintiffs are likely to succeed on at least one claim, that they would face serious, irreparable harm without a pause, and that the balance of equities and the public interest favor the injunction. The order stays in place while the court decides the full case, which was filed in April. The dispute centers on DHS waivers, issued under a 1996 border-infrastructure statute, that set aside a long list of environmental, historic-preservation, and park laws so work could move faster. The injunction does not end the broader wall program; it blocks this sector’s construction unless the government follows the laws the waivers sought to suspend, pending a final ruling.
