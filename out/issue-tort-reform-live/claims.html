Lawsuit abuse costs each Texas family about $4,600 a year in hidden costs.
factcheckableformisleading
why
The roughly $4,600 figure traces to the U.S. Chamber's Institute for Legal Reform (ILR) tort-cost study. ILR put Texas tort costs at nearly $38 billion in 2022, an average of $4,594 per household. Texans for Lawsuit Reform (TLR) and the Insurance Council of Texas repeat it. TLR's roundup rounds it to about $4,600 per household . The claim has three problems. - What the number measures. ILR's "tort costs" are defined as litigation expenses, claimant payouts and administrative fees . That covers all insurable tort costs, including legitimate claims, and is not a measure of "lawsuit abuse". ILR also says its figure is limited to insurable tort costs . - Who produced it. The estimate comes from a business-lobby group and is promoted by a tort-reform advocacy group. The study covers 2022 and is a household figure, not a family figure, so it is not current "a year" data. - Competing estimates. Other advocacy-linked estimates for "lawsuit abuse" in Texas differ. These include $1,725 per person and a Perryman Group study giving $5,549 per Texas family . A Washington Examiner op-ed puts it at $1,943 per Texas resident . Billboard lawyers profit from lawsuit abuse while Texans pay higher costs.
factcheckableformisleading
why
The claim is a rhetorical, partisan framing, and it bundles several assertions that the records don't support equally. The wording matches the Insurance Council of Texas's "Greedy Billboard Lawyers" page, which is industry advocacy. That page cites Texas leading the nation in nuclear verdicts from 2009 to 2023 ( 207 verdicts over $10 million, totaling more than $45 billion ) and estimates tort costs at about $4,594 per household . The tort-cost figures come from the U.S. Chamber of Commerce's Institute for Legal Reform, and Texans for Lawsuit Reform (TLR) repeats them. Both groups are parties to the tort-reform fight. There is some independent support for part of the claim. A Chamber of Commerce legal-reform study found Texas among the top four nuclear-verdict states, and an NAIC journal article says these verdicts can directly affect the cost and availability of insurance . Official and neutral sources do not give litigation as the main cause of Texas's rising insurance costs.
