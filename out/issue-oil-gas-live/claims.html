The IEA says global diesel shortages will last well into 2027.
factcheckablesupported
why
The IEA's October 2026 Oil Market Report (published Oct 14) has a section headed "Gasoil and diesel market tightness set to extend into 2027." The section covers diesel market tightness extending into 2027. Reuters, via BOE Report, reported that constrained diesel supplies could persist well into next year . CNBC reported that the IEA said supply would remain tight into the latter half of 2027 , and that replenishing lost inventories may not begin before the second half of 2027 . The claim's wording ("global diesel shortages will last well into 2027") is slightly firmer than the IEA's hedged language ("could persist," "likely to persist"). It also describes a global market tightness, not a Texas matter. The core attribution, the timing and the "well into 2027" phrasing all match the IEA and the news coverage, so the gist is accurate. Democrats created the cost of living crisis.
factcheckableagainstmisleading
why
The claim is a sweeping causal and partisan statement, and the records don't support it as worded. The Congressional Research Service says the 2021–22 inflation began with COVID-19 supply disruptions, was worsened by the war in Ukraine, and was also fed by "unprecedented fiscal and monetary stimulus" (https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R47273). Stimulus was passed under presidents of both parties. Economists disagree about how much each factor mattered: Richmond Fed and New York Fed work stresses supply-side factors, while an MIT Sloan summary of new research emphasizes federal spending (https://www.richmondfed.org/publications/research/econ_focus/2022/q3_feature2, https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/federal-spending-was-responsible-2022-spike-inflation-research-shows). Some Democratic-backed spending is a defensible contributor, but no source says one party "created" the crisis. For Texas specifically, the Dallas Fed attributes rising home-insurance costs to construction costs, climate risk and reinsurance costs, not to one party (https://www.dallasfed.org/research/swe/2026/swe2609). The claim also names no specific policy, so it can't be tested against the oil-and-gas [1] [2] [3] [4]
