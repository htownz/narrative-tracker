why
- Donations: NOTUS reviewed FEC filings and found that, through June, Paxton's federal political operation had received at least $448,000 from executives and political committees of companies building, operating and investing in data centers . That total spans his campaign, leadership PAC, joint fundraising committees and a pro-Paxton super PAC. It was against a war chest of $9.3 million as of June 30 , so it is roughly 5% of his haul. "Bankroll" overstates that. The donor list also covers firms such as engineering and building-materials companies. The sources I found do not tie the figure to the Houston-area or Caldwell County developers named in the context (Montera, McCord, Tract, Edged). - Tax breaks: The state sales-tax exemption for qualifying data centers is real. Qualifying data centers can avoid the 6.25% state sales tax on equipment and on electricity . The Senate Finance Committee projects it will cost about $3.3 billion in 2028–29 . The break comes from a 2013 law , not from Paxton, and it now faces bipartisan pushback. Republican Sen. Bob Hall argued that it ultimately shifts costs to other taxpayers .
