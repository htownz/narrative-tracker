Megaprojects cost too muchThe 10-year Unified Transportation Program puts huge sums into a few showpiece freeway projects such as burying I-345 in Dallas, so taxpayers should question the price tag and the priorities.

@MarcGravely

Texas's new road plan lists $1.65 billion to bury 1.4 miles of freeway in Dallas. That's more than $1 billion a mile. The road is I-345, the elevated stretch between downtown and Deep Ellum, running from I-30 to Woodall Rodgers. It's the second-largest project in the 10-year plan, which carries about $95 billion in transportation investments across the state. TxDOT's design rebuilds it below street level, in a trench with no frontage roads and new city streets crossing over the top. Dallas City Council backed that approach in May 2023. There's no start date yet. TxDOT has previously estimated about five years of construction once the money is lined up. But the reason this stretch matters starts long before the freeway: In the 1870s, two railroads met just east of downtown Dallas, and Deep Ellum grew up around the junction. It became a home of early blues. Blind Lemon Jefferson and Lead Belly played there. In 1937, Robert Johnson recorded in a makeshift studio in downtown Dallas. Then the roads came. In 1969, an elevated stretch of Central Expressway wiped out the 2400 block of Elm Street, which many saw as the heart of the neighborhood. The elevated freeway now called I-345 opened in 1973, standing between Deep Ellum and downtown. In 1973, Texas lifted a freeway over Deep Ellum. Fifty-three years later, it plans to spend $1.65 billion putting it underground.open ↗
