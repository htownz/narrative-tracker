Texas has officially banned THC.
factcheckablemisleading
why
The claim is misleading. It is broader than what the record shows. The Texas Tribune reports that after a May Texas Supreme Court ruling, DSHS can enforce a Schedule I classification of hemp-derived THC, except low-dose delta-9. That covers delta-8, delta-10, THCP and THCA flower, and enforcement resumed around July 31, 2026 ( the ruling let DSHS reclassify all hemp-derived THC except low-dose delta-9 as Schedule 1, and the ban applies to delta-8, delta-10, THCP and THCA flower ). The same reporting says delta-9 THC, the most commonly sold THC product in Texas, will remain legal . This ban also did not come from the Legislature. Abbott vetoed SB 3, the statutory ban Lt. Gov. Patrick backed, and the current restrictions come from agency rules and court rulings ( Abbott vetoed it in June 2025 ; secondary summaries say the Legislature never passed a ban). "Officially banned THC" therefore overstates the situation. Texas has banned most non-delta-9 hemp THC products, not THC in general. Marijuana was already illegal, and hemp delta-9 products at or below 0.3% remain legal for adults 21 and over. Litigation is still ongoing. Congress voted to close the federal loophole that allowed the hemp THC industry to operate, taking effect in December.
factcheckablesupported
why
Congress did vote to close the loophole. On November 12, 2025, it enacted H.R. 5371, the spending package that reopened the government, which changed the federal definition of hemp. The law moved hemp to a "total THC" standard. That closes the gap that let products high in non-delta-9 cannabinoids, such as delta-8 and THCA, count as hemp. The law originally took effect on November 12, 2026. A stopgap funding bill signed on September 2, 2026 pushed most of the new restrictions back 30 days. That makes the main date December 11, 2026. Products containing cannabinoids the plant can't naturally produce still lose hemp status on November 12. A Yahoo Finance report from October 5, 2026 also says the potency limit is set to take effect in December . The claim's wording is loose in two ways. It implies a recent vote, when the vote was in 2025, and the December delay came from a later vote. It also leaves out the narrower November 12 date for synthetic cannabinoids. But the core statement is accurate: Congress passed the closure, and most of it takes effect in December. [1] [2] [3] [4]
