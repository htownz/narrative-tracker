Foreign buyers are the reason homes are so expensive in America.
factcheckableagainstunsupported
why
NAR's 2026 International Transactions report (published July 29, 2026) counts 67,100 foreign-buyer purchases of existing homes between April 2025 and March 2026, the second-lowest level since NAR began tracking in 2009 . That is about 1.7% of existing-home purchases . Most of those purchases (37,600, or 56%) were by recent immigrants or visa holders already living in the U.S. , so buyers based abroad were fewer still. In Texas, 12% of foreign purchasers bought there , and the Texas Tribune's coverage of the comptroller's report describes a bipartisan consensus that affordability problems stem from a shortage of homes . The Comptroller's report points to supply that hasn't kept up with demand , higher interest rates and zoning and land-use rules that limit development . Newsweek's reporting likewise says supply, construction levels, zoning and mortgage rates are much larger drivers of affordability than foreign ownership . Foreign buyers can affect prices in some local markets, but a share this small does not support calling them "the reason" homes are expensive nationally. One caveat is that NAR's figures come from a member survey and may be imperfect. [1] [2] [3] [4]
