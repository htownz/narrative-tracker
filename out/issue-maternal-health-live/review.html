@LunaForTruth How many deaths versus births have happened since the abortion ban in Texas Texas’s early-pregnancy abortion ban (SB 8) took effect September 1, 2021, and a near-total ban followed after Dobbs in 2022. Research comparing observed outcomes with expected trends finds thousands of additional births and a much smaller rise in infant and maternal deaths. Births A Johns Hopkins study in JAMA estimated that SB 8 was associated with 9,799 additional births in Texas from April through December 2022 (297,088 observed vs. 287,289 expected under a synthetic-control counterfactual)—about a 3% increase. The pattern was not seen in other states. A University of Houston analysis of CDC data reported more than 16,000 more births in 2022 than in 2021 (about a 2% rise in the fertility rate), with larger increases among Hispanic women. Broader work on states with bans estimated roughly 22,000 more births than expected across those states, with Texas accounting for a large share. Later access to out-of-state care and mailed medication abortion has reduced the share of pregnancies carried to term that would otherwise have been aborted. Infant deaths Infant deaths in Texas rose from 1,985 in 2021 to 2,240 in 2022 (+255, or 12.9%), versus a 1.8% rise in the rest of the U.S. A JAMA Pediatrics counterfactual analysis estimated about 216 excess infant deaths from March to December 2022 (12.7% above expectation; confidence interval wide and including zero at the low end). The increase was concentrated in congenital anomalies (up about 23% in Texas while falling slightly elsewhere)—conditions often diagnosed after six weeks and previously a common reason for abortion. A later multi-state analysis attributed most of an estimated 478 excess infant deaths in ban states to Texas (about 384). Maternal deaths These numbers are far smaller. ProPublica’s analysis of Texas hospital data found 79 in-hospital deaths of pregnant or postpartum women in 2018–2019 versus 120 in 2022–2023. CDC-based analyses report the Texas maternal mortality rate up about 33% from 2019 to 2023 while the national rate fell about 7.5%; one analysis put the 2022 rate increase at 56%. Sepsis among women hospitalized for second-trimester pregnancy loss rose more than 50% after the ban. Absolute maternal deaths remain on the order of dozens per year in a state with roughly 370,000–400,000 births annually. Scale In the first post-ban birth window that has been studied carefully, additional births were on the order of ~10,000, excess infant deaths in the low hundreds, and excess maternal deaths in the tens. Studies treat the ban as associated with these shifts after accounting for other states’ trends, but they do not claim every extra birth or death is caused only by the law; COVID-era disruptions, care access, and continued out-of-state or medication abortions all affect the totals.open ↗
