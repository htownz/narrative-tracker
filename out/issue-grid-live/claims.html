ERCOT's large-load queue grew from 63 GW to 474 GW in about 18 months, more than five times the roughly 91 GW record peak demand, with only a small share approved.
factcheckablesupported
why
ERCOT's own filings support each figure in the claim. The December 2025 board materials put the large-load queue at about 226 GW, compared to 63 GW in December 2024 . ERCOT's July 29, 2026 Senate presentation shows approximately 474 GW of Large Loads seeking interconnection, of which ~90% are data centers , with data as of June 2026. That is about 18 months from the December 2024 baseline. The record peak is 91.1 GW, set on July 22, 2026 ( EIA ). ERCOT lists it as unofficial until settlement, so 474/91 is about 5.2 times. On the approved share, ERCOT-derived figures show roughly 9 to 9.5 GW approved to energize, about 2% of the queue ( 9,456 MW approved to energize per ERCOT's August operational overview, as reported by DC Hub ). Separately, ERCOT's Senate slides show many projects excluded from Batch Zero for lacking a qualifying study, and the larger remainder has no approval. The claim's wording is loose in places: "approved" is not defined, and the queue is requests, not demand. The 63 GW, 474 GW, 91 GW and "small share approved" figures all match the sources. Data center queue is speculative bubble 3

Texas has paused or frozen new data center grid connections and approvals.
factcheckablesupported
why
Gov. Abbott's Aug. 3, 2026 directive ordered the PUCT and ERCOT to audit data centers in ERCOT's interconnection queue. ERCOT said it was pausing its batch zero review. ERCOT's Sept. 11 board presentation says it paused approvals to energize data centers or crypto facilities of 75 MW or greater . Abbott's Sept. 21 order also pauses permit approvals for data centers across all state agencies until the audit is complete . The pause is limited. ERCOT said it could only stop connections for loads of at least 75 MW, so smaller data centers and projects building their own power plants were not included . One analysis calls it "not a moratorium on data center development." The pause is also temporary, tied to the audit, which ERCOT expects to finish around December. The claim's wording "paused or frozen" is a fair summary of the grid-connection pause for large data centers (≥75 MW) in ERCOT. It is slightly imprecise in implying a blanket, permanent freeze on all connections. I found nothing showing the pause has been lifted as of Oct. 14, 2026. [1] [2] [3] [4]
